I am a postdoctoral associate in the [Jeehwan Kim group](https://jeehwanlab.mit.edu/) at MIT, working on **membrane-based integrated photonics**.

Remote epitaxy grows a single-crystalline film on a substrate through an intervening two-dimensional layer, so the film can afterward be peeled off and handled as a freestanding membrane. That changes which materials a photonic circuit is allowed to contain: a complex oxide can sit on silicon, or on top of a finished CMOS wafer, without ever having to be grown there. I design the active devices that follow, electro-optic and light-emitting, from the optics and the electrostatics through to a process flow a cleanroom will actually run.

Before MIT I did my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at GIST with [Young Min Song](https://www.gist-foel.net/), in the Flexible OptoElectronics Laboratory. The subject was ultra-thin film resonators. A few tens of nanometers of absorbing dielectric on a mirror forms a Gires-Tournois cavity, and its resonance is sharp enough to read single bioparticles out of an ordinary bright-field micrograph. The same structure, retuned, gives structural color displays, colorimetric sensors, and daytime radiative coolers.

Most of my work sits on the boundary between simulation and fabrication: FDTD, mode solving, drift-diffusion, and finite-element modeling, each constrained by what the process can hold.
